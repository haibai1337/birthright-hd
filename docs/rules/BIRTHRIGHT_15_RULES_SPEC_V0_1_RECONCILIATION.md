# Birthright 1.5 v0.1 — Technical reconciliation

Reviewed **2026-10-24**. **Research and planning only; no implementation authorized.**

Inputs: [proposed specification v0.1](BIRTHRIGHT_15_RULES_SPEC_V0_1.md), [holdings audit](BIRTHRIGHT_15_HOLDINGS_AUDIT.md), [economic cases](BIRTHRIGHT_15_HOLDINGS_ECONOMIC_CASES.md) and [source register](BIRTHRIGHT_15_HOLDINGS_SOURCES.md). Historical labels B30, B35 and PC-H follow the register. B30 page references are printed pages, also one-based PDF pages; B35 references identify the chapter and one-based PDF page.

**Finding:** v0.1 is a usable statement of the selected direction, but is not yet an executable rules contract or a validated Anuire economy. J01–J08 are confirmed. The remaining work is to reconcile dependent formulas, supply character conversions and cost complete faction ledgers. This review does not substitute the audit's preferred Package A for the user's choices.

Four corrections matter immediately: provincial base income is already verified; the later bloodline chapter conflicts with chapter 5; the court table overlaps at level 4; and representative budgets alone cannot establish complete Anuire coverage.

Three historical uncertainties must stay visible:

- **Bloodline:** B30 ch. 2 pp. 36–37 agrees with chapter 5's 2B collection/5B reserve. B35 ch. 2 pp. 8–9 instead uses a doubled score, B collection/2B reserve, different growth costs and different sacrifice units. B35 ch. 5 p. 8 retains older 2B/5B wording. Thus the audit D02 and spec §6 overgeneralize when they attribute one coherent cap package to “B30/B35.” See §3 below.
- **Court brackets:** B35 ch. 5 p. 4, Table 5-5, visibly prints Average **3–4** with one court action per round and Good **4–5** with two. Level 4 is ambiguous. The remaining printed brackets grant three at 6–7, four at 8–9 and five at 10+. A correction requires a declared 1.5 choice; do not silently infer it from a formula.
- **Maintenance/attitudes:** Table 5-4 supports province-fort upkeep 2/3 GB per level and holding-fort upkeep 1/3; inherited warfare prose conflicts. The attitude table also has a disputed duplicated threshold. These are already flagged in the audit/register, not resolved by choosing the newest edition. The [publisher version record](https://forum.birthright.net/t/brcs-3-5e-pdf-ruleset-chapters-developed-by-birthright-net/35907) confirms that chapters 6–9 have no separate 3.5 revision and that v1.1 does not incorporate all wiki errata.

## 3. Missing character and bloodline requirements

### Character skills and actors

Current [character schema](../../packages/core/schema.ts) stores classes/levels, bloodline, inventory and command/diplomacy attributes; it has no BRCS skill-rank record. The [Anuire adapter](../../packages/world-data/anuire-scenario.ts) explicitly sets those two attributes to **unused zero sentinels**. They cannot be read as zero ranks, real ability scores or evidence of incompetence.

The minimum RP conversion needs the **13 named skill categories** used by the five ratings: Diplomacy, Warcraft, Lead, Knowledge (Religion/Arcana/Nature), Appraise, Bluff, Craft, Gather Information, Intimidate, Profession and Sense Motive. Craft and Profession require named specialties; the formula uses one of each, not all specialties added together. Zero, unknown and not applicable must remain distinct. Ordinary BRCS collection also requires a bloodline; skills alone do not grant unblooded rulers RP, and the optional level-based variant is not selected automatically.

An additional omission is **Administrate**, which is outside the RP rating table but affects the selected economy/action framework. B35 ch. 1 PDF pp. 22–24 defines Administrate, Lead, Warcraft and skill synergies. Administrate offers a 25% maintenance reduction with a round-down instruction, checked at DC 10 + base maintenance in GB. It can also save 1 GB on a standard action costing at least 2 GB, checked at DC 20 − planned cost; it does not make a 1 GB Rule action free. These are evidenced options, **not automatically selected discounts**. The discount's eligible cost base, check timing, actor and retry limit need definition before it enters budgets. [Primary chapter 1 PDF](https://forum.birthright.net/uploads/short-url/A2p8Enx1s0qMjoOB9vaSyDAtL8o.pdf).

For each actual rules actor, the conversion worksheet needs:

1. Stable regent/deployment IDs, selected ruler or lieutenant role, verified original classes/levels, race/culture and spell eligibility; an evidence/confidence field for each derived value.
2. RP-relevant ranks, specialties, and the separate ranks/ability modifiers needed by adopted action checks. A +2 synergy or feat bonus to a check is not two extra RP-collection ranks.
3. A defensible allocation method: compatible class/cross-class skills, level/rank limits, available skill points and multiclass treatment, or an explicitly approved simpler **1.5 conversion**. Original class identity is evidence, not a license to manufacture optimal scores. Full RPG character creation is not required merely to supply domain fields.
4. Actor limits: who can conduct a standard/full action, add a personal skill bonus, cast a realm spell or substitute as lieutenant, and which pool pays. A historical candidate becoming a lieutenant does not create a second domain treasury or court.
5. Explicit collection rounding: fractional Guild rating, percentage entitlement across equivalent holdings, final whole RP and cap order. Aggregate before rounding; never award the same control twice for multiclass membership or duplicate original records.

Inventory all **116 regent records**, assigning conversion priority to active rulers, every offered starting alternative and usable lieutenants. Preserve unresolved/inactive/controller records as such; do not fabricate ranks merely to fill a table. Class and spell identities can be recovered from original evidence; BRCS ranks absent from a 1997 ruleset generally require a disclosed conversion rather than “recovery.”

Use different symbols in the conversion contract: **A** = preserved AD&D/native numerical score; **b** = B30 ability-scale score; **S** = B35 chapter-2 doubled score. Numerical score and minor/major/great/true grade are different properties; the current field named `bloodline.strength` carries the numerical value.

For comparison only, A70 → b35 gives collection cap 70 and reserve 175; a **hypothetical** S70 interpretation gives collection 70 and reserve 140. Applying chapter 5's 2B/5B directly to S70 instead gives 140/350. The same skill table therefore produces very different strategic reserves, Alchemy capacity, RP bidding and emergency solvency. The reserve difference survives even when collection limits happen to match.

J05 confirms skill sensitivity, **not a choice among these bloodline packages**. Recommend presenting a complete scale/cap/growth package for final judgment, with odd native scores, unblooded characters, high-score awnsheghlien, current reserves and vassal transfers explicitly covered. Do not clamp unusual original scores to the extent of a printed table. Growth, forced sacrifice and optional level-based caps are separate selections; J07/J08 do not authorize invisible bloodline loss.

Any eventual conversion should be a versioned derived overlay keyed to original IDs. Preserve raw numerical scores, derivation, grade, abilities and initial RP. Decide old-save opt-in/compatibility separately; no rescaling or reserve truncation follows from this review. Existing campaigns must not be silently converted into a newly capped economy.

## 4. Economic compatibility and counterexamples

### Law and Seizure

Passive Law is **new ordinary production** in the selected package. With P4 and Law/Guild/Temple all at 4, combined ordinary production is 4+4/3+8/3+8/3 = **32/3 GB**, before virtual cash, ports, routes and separately payable costs. It is legitimate for world production to rise with this choice. A world ledger must identify that increase, not disguise it as a transfer or subtract it a second time from the sovereign. Audit D03/D04 and economic Cases 3–4 remain relevant to the _additional_ leverage of Law.

Seizure must use one bounded actual-receipt pool with matching payer/recipient entries. Adding legacy automatic Claims would violate J02. Court expansion, multiple Law records, allied claimants and orders from several factions must not each seize the same receipts. Define whether the proposed seasonal repeat limit belongs to the asset, victim owner/province or claimant-target pair; the last alone can still permit coordinated repeated extraction. Fort immunity, legal opposition and diplomatic consequences must be priced together. The BRCS paragraph supports proportional division; it does not supply a complete opposed domain action.

### Virtual Guilds, trade and magic

J04's cash formula is settled: V=max(Hsource−4,0), cash=2V/3. H7 yields **2 GB**, not another independent Guild holding or Guild RP. What remains is the entitlement contract: aggregation before the threshold, suppressed/destroyed Source status, imported excesses, non-caster ownership, access through another mage's ley line, and eligibility for non-trade Guild actions. Remote access must not accidentally duplicate a Source's commercial income at every network endpoint. Preserve original Source ownership while deciding who can exercise its benefits.

J03's real-Guild condition is a deliberate restriction of BRCS virtual trade rights. Test virtual cash alongside the existing Alchemy candidate: 10 RP converted at 1 RP/GB, with a 1 GB fee, yields **9 net GB** and consumes an action. Expanded court/lieutenant casting must not turn that scarce casting opportunity into unlimited conversions. Higher RP reserves, stronger skill collection and cheaper growth all change its practical supply. Do not assume an infinite loop already exists; demonstrate whether every proposed cycle consumes bounded RP, actions, time or other inputs. Cases 7–8 and 11 define useful counterexamples.

Trade must count **both** endpoint shares and their actual owners once. A real-Guild contract must identify authority, revocation and capacity. Higher real Guild levels can increase ordinary income, each route's share and route capacity together, so growth and court throughput must be tested with trade, not independently. Sea-route forecasts require a costed port/shipping policy; absent verified fleets are not free unlimited cargo capacity. Retain the approved full-level seaport and half-level river-port income with fractions. Current HD includes port income in its `trade` total and then grants thieves RP from that total: under J03/J05, the old trade-derived RP addition must not be inherited accidentally. Cases 5–6 apply.

### Net collections, maintenance and affordable courts

Removing legacy administration is required, but is insufficient to establish net solvency. Publish one cost dictionary covering armies and home garrisons, fort type/level, occupation, court, salaries, roads/bridges, ports/shipyards, fleets, spells/actions and contractual transfers. Charge each supported asset once. Table 5-4, military payroll and the conflicting fortification prose are alternative evidence to reconcile, not cumulative bills.

Court costs expose a real compatibility problem. At the evidenced 1 GB/level price, an isolated real Guild 4 or Temple 4 earns 8/3 GB but a court 4 costs 4: **−4/3 GB before other expenses**. Law 4 yields 4/3 against the same court cost: **−8/3 GB**. BRCS expects a court near the largest non-Source holding, so that expectation cannot be imposed universally without testing independent institutions, patronage and smaller courts. A Source 7 earns 2 GB virtually but Sources are excluded from that historical court expectation. These are examples, not findings that any named faction is insolvent.

Current HD has court tiers 0–4 priced 0/1/3/6/9 GB, flat fort support per fort, original unit-profile payroll and count-based administration. A preserved native court tier is not automatically the same-numbered BRCS court level: starting-court conversion needs an explicit derived mapping, without rewriting its original field. Native NPC paths also contain payroll exemptions, RP/cash fallback and a tiny cash floor. None is automatically the normalized 1.5 policy. Complete budgets must show these current exceptions separately and specify which ordinary/special-controller policies the proposed model assumes; they cannot be used to conceal a structural deficit. J08 forbids solving player-mode deficits with Basic grants or immunity.

Precision is a rules/storage dependency. Current `gold` validation and `goldBars` require/round to tenths; thirds cannot survive unchanged. Thirds and half-port income require more precision; BRCS infrastructure and military candidates also contain quarters, twelfths and naval twenty-fourths. **120 units per GB** represents those fractions plus existing tenths, before additional discounts/multipliers; it is not a universal solution to every possible formula. Rational arithmetic or a sufficiently justified exact scale is a technical choice. Round only at a declared boundary, never separately per preserved record. No schema or save edit is authorized now.

### Court throughput, growth and settlement timing

One extra court action per round means **three extra opportunities per season**; five means fifteen. They are restricted opportunities, not fifteen arbitrary standard actions. With a standard Rule action supported by court actions, each additional target needs its own eligibility, cost and check. Reducing Rule to 1 GB while removing fixed target-level RP, then adding cheap delegation, can accelerate income growth and two-stage suppression dramatically. A second contest success must have a defined earliest round and recovery window. Assimilation, landless vassals and spare character records cannot mint courts/standard slots. Compare action capacity, spend and resulting income together (audit D08/D18; Case 9 and the court-factory checklist).

Spec §8's proposed RP-before-expenses sequence differs from HD, which can spend existing RP on payroll before adding the new collection. Whether freshly collected RP can prevent desertion is an economic decision. Also define Seizure reservation versus collection date, paid pledges versus collection/reserve caps, spell collection rights, fort damage and before/after-tax pillage. Current code suggests a collection-spell expiry risk; it remains a reproduction task, not a freshly demonstrated regression (Case 10).

Basic can resolve a legal allocation/default automatically and record it. Advanced must obtain the selected confirmation; Expert waits for explicit allocation/default decisions. **Identical choices must produce identical ledgers in all three modes.** Accountability does not require making Basic wait for manual confirmation, and automation does not create new resources.

### Minimum work to validate complete Anuire budgets

Spec §11 currently requires representative opening/mature ledgers. That is useful but weaker than **complete faction validation**. The minimum extension is a read-only accounting study, not a long AI balance campaign or a rules implementation:

1. **Freeze and reconcile the inputs.** Record hashes/versions of the existing campaign and research sidecars. Account for all **51 domains, 206 provinces, 766 holding records, 39 fort records and 297 deployments (234 military, 63 character)** in the retained native dataset. Join original IDs, ruler candidates, raw court/treasury/RP, military profiles, ports, diplomacy/tribute and supported magic. Retain duplicate/excess records and unknowns. The verified initialization sidecar converts native treasury tenths once for the analytical opening balance; it must not rescale a saved campaign or rewrite the source. Unknown original routes/infrastructure are not invented to make budgets balance.
2. **Prepare a complete parameter sheet.** Use the verified collections now; enumerate unresolved cost, court, taxation, trade, RP and calendar parameters. Include an explicit no-extra-discount baseline and sensitivity alternatives for unselected Administrate rules. Classify each domain as ordinary active, holding-only, passive/inactive or special controller using current contracts. Label player versus NPC control, enabled event policies and the opening collection boundary so comparisons do not mix different exemptions or timings. Give every record a row, including economically excluded controllers with an explanation; do not treat Chaos as an ordinary taxable player or eliminate a holding-only institution.
3. **Build one static season ledger for every faction.** Show opening GB, province/Law/Guild/Temple/virtual/port production separately, route shares, incoming/outgoing GB/RP transfers, Seizure, each expense head, committed action/casting spend, unpaid obligations and closing GB/RP. Reconcile opening + production + transfers + conversions − spending = closing. Show both current HD expected income (with its conditional dice/claims ranges and NPC exceptions) and the proposed deterministic ordinary income. Unknowns must be marked, not quietly entered as zero. A paired spreadsheet or research calculation can do this without calling gameplay commands.
4. **Cost the actual regent variants.** Cover every active faction's actual ruler and every offered starting alternative: both candidates in the 18 Classic kingdoms, plus candidates exposed by supported expanded access. A single “warrior/merchant/mage” default is insufficient. Complete RP certification waits for approved skill/bloodline overlays; provisional lower/upper bounds may expose risks but cannot certify final RP solvency. Do not require the full cross-product of all other kingdoms' ruler choices where a row is independent; document dependencies where trade/pledges make it matter.
5. **Add bounded opening and developed-season cases.** For every ordinary active faction, include the actual opening assets and an explicit lawful developed-state/action plan; retain all original records and mark new development as hypothetical. Cost growth, court expansion, routes, maintenance and magic instead of granting completed development for free. Add a small stress set covering a holding-only merchant, temple, mage and Law institution, weak/strong bloodlines, blockade, war/occupation, suppression, loss of a partner and a shortfall. Use matched turns/boundaries to reveal liquidity and reserve depletion. These cases need no M1D personality simulation and do not promise long-run campaign balance.
6. **Close the accounting and exploit checks.** Reconcile every bilateral transfer/route across factions; compare total production with the selected Law/virtual/port additions; verify each payroll/fort/court/road/vessel charge once. Test record splitting, zero-level routes, repeated coordinated Seizure, shared shipping, Alchemy/RP/pledge cycles and court/lieutenant multiplication. Trace at least one boundary with an income spell and one insolvent settlement. For a fixed set of choices, Basic/Advanced/Expert must have the same numerical result; only decision delegation differs. Report any necessary asset loss or subsidy explicitly for judgment.

The reviewable output should be **a 51-domain coverage ledger, a regent conversion/gap matrix, one versioned parameter sheet and a short exceptions/sensitivity report**. These are the minimum next research deliverables, not artifacts claimed to exist in this review. A faction with a missing material cost, unapproved subsidy, unknown skill entitlement or unresolved bloodline package remains provisional. A negative budget is a finding to explain, not permission to change its original assets.

Implementation readiness requires coverage of every faction/controller, balanced transfer entries, approved material parameters and conversions, affordable and bounded action plans, and an explicit future rules/save-version strategy. Representative examples, total-world profit or a successful browser build cannot replace that evidence. M1B remains partial; this report changes no game rules, campaign records, saves or UI.

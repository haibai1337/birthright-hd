# Birthright 1.5 — Complete Holdings Rules Audit

**Research and recommendations only — 2026-10-24. No mechanics are selected, implemented or rewritten by this document.** This audit supersedes the assumption that M1B economic gaps should immediately be filled with one historical version. Further major domain-economic changes should follow a coherent 1.5 specification chosen by the user.

The comparison covers the 1995 AD&D boxed-set rules, Sierra's 1997 game, the January 2003 BRCS playtest, later BRCS 3.5 chapter revisions and the current HD code. It covers Law, Guild, Temple and Source together with the systems that determine their value. "Complete" describes the requested scope of this audit, not verified retail equivalence or the absence of unresolved evidence.

Read with the [source/version register](BIRTHRIGHT_15_HOLDINGS_SOURCES.md) and [worked economic cases and exploit analysis](BIRTHRIGHT_15_HOLDINGS_ECONOMIC_CASES.md). All **P15** rules below are proposals for judgment. Existing explicit HD decisions, especially port income, source-record preservation and holding-only survival, remain in force unless the user changes them.

## 1. Findings that should govern the decision

1. **Ownership of land and ownership of institutions must remain independent.** A count, a rival church, a merchant prince and a wizard can all matter in the same province. Conquest must not automatically erase the other three domains.
2. **There are two materially different cash economies.** E2/Sierra/HD use province-sensitive dice and external Law claims. BRCS uses predictable level-based collections, direct Law income and a different maintenance/action economy. Combining their most generous individual benefits is not a neutral modernization.
3. **Law's political leverage is itself a valuable return.** Routine income, seizure, tax protection, opposition, recruitment and occupation are separate advantages. Law should not receive all of them at full strength without paying an economic or political price.
4. **Trade is the largest obvious expansion loop.** Current HD permits a level-zero origin guild and derives revenue from province levels. The merchant can obtain significant recurring cash before developing a real business. Holding-based throughput is a stronger alternative, but the whole route budget, ownership, RP and upkeep package must be specified together.
5. **Sources have a distinct resource economy.** Their apparent poverty funds magical independence and leverage. BRCS virtual guilds, cheap Alchemy, free ley maintenance and broad political access can jointly eliminate that distinction.
6. **Record preservation must not create extra production.** Separate imported/assimilated holding records must retain IDs, but identical economic control should not earn more just because its records were never merged.
7. **Seasonal timing is a rule.** Snapshot date, damage date, spell expiry, tax roll, RP award and payment priority can change the result as much as the income table itself.
8. **The later BRCS is a qualified composite.** The publisher identifies revised 3.5 chapters 1–5 with older chapters 6–9; many holding rules examined here already occur in 2003. Calling an unchanged rule "the 3.5 fix" would be misleading. [Version evidence](https://forum.birthright.net/t/brcs-3-5e-pdf-ruleset-chapters-developed-by-birthright-net/35907).

**Preferred direction:** retain Sierra's strategic rhythm, named domain identities, class-based RP, readable holding levels and reversible political conflict. Add explicit conservation of transfers, substantive guild prerequisites, typed political influence and versioned seasonal accounting. Treat income normalization, routine Law funding and source-network costs as package decisions, with the candidates and tradeoffs below. Do not adopt a complete edition merely because it is earlier or later.

## 2. How to read the comparison

E2, PC-H/PC-S/PC-B, B30, B35, HD and P15 are defined in the source register. PC-H is intended behavior from original help; PC-S is qualified modified-source evidence; PC-B is a narrow static executable observation. A help/source disagreement remains visible. No original executable was run.

Notation: **P** province level; **H** holding level; **B** the bloodline value in the particular ruleset; **RP** Regency Points; **GB** seasonal domain wealth. BRCS's bloodline ability-score scale must not be substituted directly for the imported AD&D numerical bloodline. A three-month domain turn contains three action rounds. Net income, gross collections, available treasury and personal gp are distinct quantities.

Historical rule statements are deliberately concise. Exact tables and full action descriptions remain in their cited primary texts; this report supplies locators, comparisons and original analysis rather than reproducing the books. A dash means absence in the inspected core rule, not impossibility in all supplements or campaigns.

### D01 — The income model is inseparable from maintenance

**Rule/status:** `economy.ts::holdingDice/income/domainMaintenance` implements the PC-style gross receipts and a separate administration bill. BRCS chapter 5's later [expense interpretation and known conflict](https://birthright.net/wiki/index.php/Errata) say ordinary income-producing administration is already in net collections. Current HD has not implemented that net-income model.

**Consequences:** changing Guild/Temple to 2H/3 is not simply "more predictable." It raises some weak frontier incomes but lowers many developed ones. Retaining the old administration bill on top may double-charge costs that the adopted rate already absorbs. Removing that bill while retaining the higher PC dice can instead create excess surplus. Cheap recurring income funds army maintenance, action bids and spell purchases, so all three are affected.

**Advantages/disadvantages:** deterministic collections improve planning and reduce seasonal bankruptcy variance; dice preserve Sierra's uncertainty, province development value and familiar forecasts. Predictability can also make optimal expansion too mechanical; randomness can punish a small church or guilder with no reserve buffer.

**P15 recommendation:** use the existing PC dice plus explicit administration as the initial candidate, with a clearly declared budget and pure forecasts. Evaluate a separate normalized net-income candidate rather than mixing the two. If normalization wins, reprice armies, courts, forts, ports and action costs using real Anuire budgets before adopting it. This preference follows the existing campaign scale and interaction costs, not the age of E2.

### D02 — Who receives RP and how much

**Rule/status:** E2 Table 14 distinguishes classes. HD implements full Law for fighters/paladins/rangers; half rounded up for priests/thieves; full Guild for thieves/rangers, half for bards; Temple for priests/paladins; Source for wizards; provincial RP for eligible sovereigns. Trade contributes for thieves. These are not universal RP per GB. `selectors.ts::provinceDomainPower/domainPower` and `bloodlines.ts` resolve the selected ruler. B30 and B35 instead use key-skill ratings; the later table's example must use the table's percentage, as its [errata](https://birthright.net/wiki/index.php/Errata) explains.

**Consequences:** a skill-based conversion requires complete and credible character skill records. Inventing optimal ranks for imported regents would alter their strength while pretending to be rules fidelity. A multiclass character must not collect the same holding twice. Changing bloodline scale without changing reserve/collection/growth rules can multiply RP and cheapen every opposed action.

**Advantages/disadvantages:** class entitlement gives recognizable warrior/law, thief/guild, priest/temple and wizard/source identities; skill ratings allow unusual rulers to specialize and make training matter. Skill thresholds introduce optimization and missing-data problems; class gates can make a competent off-class ruler mechanically awkward.

**P15:** preserve class-based collection and selected-regent identity for the initial Anuire specification. Represent off-class income and ownership separately from RP eligibility. Aggregate eligible levels before fractional RP rounding. Keep collection cap, reserve cap, vassal transfers, voluntary bloodline growth and emergency bloodline sacrifice as separately selected policies; never import only a generous BRCS cap. A future skill system needs genuine data and a campaign migration proposal.

For an exact B30/B35 numerical comparison, Tables 5-9/10 use these collection ratings: Law = Lead + Warcraft; Temple = Knowledge (Religion) + Lead; Source = Knowledge (Arcana) + Knowledge (Nature); Guild = one quarter of the combined ranks in Appraise, Bluff, one Craft, Diplomacy, Gather Information, Intimidate, one Profession and Sense Motive. Province = Diplomacy + Warcraft. Ratings 5 or less / 6 / 7 / 8 / 9 / 10+ correspond to 0 / 20 / 40 / 60 / 80 / 100 percent; the table prints 5 as its bottom row. Source/Temple additionally require the relevant spellcasting. Both inspected chapter 5 texts cap normal seasonal collection at 2B and stored RP at 5B on **their** bloodline scale. HD implements neither this skill table nor those scale-dependent caps.

### D03 — Law is not just a revenue holding

**Rule/status:** PC-H §§127–129 gives tax/occupation loyalty protection and claims; §114 gives a separate own-occupation holding-reduction tool. HD implements ordinary protection and claims, but own occupation/reduction remains unavailable. BRCS gives Law influence over Guild/Temple creation, development and contest, with direct revenue plus exceptional seizure. See [action rules](https://birthright.net/wiki/index.php/Contest_holding), B30 pp. 107–110 and B35 PDF pp. 18–21. Current HD's influence is broader and less typed than that relationship.

**Consequences:** an independent sheriff needs support without automatically becoming richer than the merchant he regulates. Unpriced tax immunity plus safe recurring claims can turn Law into the best economic investment. Conversely, a lawful regent with claims disabled may have political duties but no GB stream.

**P15:** keep political enforcement and loyalty protection central. Prefer negotiated Law funding from an identified payer over a new unbacked revenue stream. A candidate service allocation is `min(available provincial revenue, active Law level / 3)` GB, transferred from that province's receipts under an agreement; **this is a new P15 funding proposal, not the literal BRCS H/3 rule**. Multiple Law owners share a bounded budget; the province ruler's own allocation is internal accounting, not new world income. Compare it with explicit fixed tribute and the normalized direct-income alternative. No funding proposal is approved here.

### D04 — Routine funding, legal claims and predatory seizure

**Exact distinctions:** E2 Table 18A keys random claims to relative levels **and actual target receipts**. PC-H §129 gives a bounded but incomplete description. HD instead computes `min(remaining receipts, 3, floor(target level × ratio))`, with ratios 1/2, 3/4 or 1 by relative Law level, iterating separate Law records. BRCS's exceptional seizure is a different instrument; it is not the missing PC probability distribution. [Collections/seizures](https://birthright.net/wiki/index.php/BRCS%3AChapter_five/Ruling_a_domain/Domain_collections), B30 p. 97, B35 PDF p. 9.

**Advantages/disadvantages:** an automatic claim is easy to administer but can become permanent parasitism. Exceptional seizure creates dramatic politics but is exploitable if repeatable without opposition, reputation loss or a real victim debit. Fortification immunity changes the return on defensive construction.

**P15:** choose one explicit ordinary funding channel per targeted income stream, and a separate extraordinary seizure order with a meaningful action cost, opposition, public diplomatic consequences and a once-per-target-season limit. Every payment debits actual uncommitted target receipts; never a treasury balance merely because it exists. Self-claims net to zero. Aggregate duplicate Law records before claim caps, use proportional allocation for simultaneous claimants, and exclude Source RP/Alchemy reserves from a generic "taxable holding" selector. Exact seizure size is a user decision after the cases; do not silently bless today's formula.

### D05 — Guild and Temple have equal cash tables, different strategic jobs

**Rule/status:** all four inspected historical layers use equal base Guild/Temple collection within their own model. HD does too. Guilds additionally support routes; temples support divine realm magic, investiture politics and religious agitation. In BRCS, the later Agitate/attitude action is not Sierra's one-grade d20 attempt. HD exposes casting/ley/blood controls and ordinary Agitate; independent faith policy, full intelligence, character acquisition and full investiture opposition are incomplete. See PC-H §§144/150/168/231; B30/B35 named Agitate, Ceremony, Create Trade Route and Cast Realm Spell headings.

**Advantages/disadvantages:** equal base cash avoids arbitrary religious poverty; extra trade can make Guild the richer option, while Temple has political/magical leverage. Importing all guild intelligence bonuses but omitting priestly leverage would produce a one-sided comparison. Temple (3) is an institution's share of worship, not three standalone churches that automatically share another faith's spells.

**P15:** preserve equal base collections. Differentiate Guild through contractual commerce/intelligence, Temple through religion/loyalty/divine magic and investiture services. Preserve the priest's once-seasonal free Agitate candidate, but do not also grant free BRCS court amplification or unlimited multi-province agitation. Require distinct faith ownership and explicit religious diplomatic relationships; no automatic universal church merger. A temple owner need not be an eligible caster, and owning a province need not give control of its temples.

### D06 — Sources, virtual guilds and caster eligibility

**Rule/status:** the core source collection line is zero. Both inspected BRCS chapter 5 versions add virtual guild behavior at high source levels: B30 p. 103; B35 PDF p. 15. This is not a new 3.5 invention or the same as every later AD&D supplement variant. Source privacy and restricted political participation are also part of that design. [Source action discussion](https://birthright.net/wiki/index.php/BRCS%3AChapter_five/Ruling_a_domain/Domain_action_rounds).

Their explicit exception starts at Source 4 as virtual Guild 0, then increases one-for-one: virtual Guild level `H − 4`. It produces Guild-like GB and action access, but not extra Guild RP, and does not occupy normal Guild capacity or exist as a separately contestable/transferrable holding. That exception must accompany any claim that the BRCS source economy is simply cashless.

E2 p. 82/Table 24 uses a different device: source-level tiers bar particular domain actions, with level 0 barred from all and level 7+ having no listed prohibition. The intermediate tiers distinguish trade and several mundane political/building/mustering actions; the OCR's column alignment needs checking against page images before implementing those exact tier assignments. PC retail equivalence of those restrictions is not established by having recovered Source records. HD's general local-holding checks do not implement either that tier table or the later virtual-guild substitution.

HD has no virtual-guild income, but `holdingInfluence` can use any local holding/fort for political Contest/Agitate and source creation/development is not comprehensively gated by true-magic eligibility. `casterProfile` accepts several arcane classes; `isTrueWizard` includes magician. Those are implementation facts to audit, not proof that all such characters ought to have full Birthright realm magic.

**Consequences:** a source that also produces unopposed Guild income outside the normal Guild pool can bypass both merchant competition and the landless mage's financing problem. Combined with cheap Alchemy and no network costs, its owner may get RP, money, warfare and political influence from the same investment. Ordinary troops destroying sources would erase the converse magical independence.

**P15:** initially retain zero passive Source GB and keep real commercial holdings/paid patronage available. Reject a free virtual-guild layer in the first specification. Require explicit true-realm-magic eligibility for creating/ruling/casting through Sources, preserve imported ownership without deletion, and separate ownership, collection and casting checks. Sources should not automatically supply full secular political influence. Spell acquisition, resistance and each class exception need their own evidence and decision. See D15 for network costs and the cases for Alchemy conversion.

### D07 — Presence is not ownership of the whole province

**Consequence:** fixed sovereign creation resistance makes institutional entry into a developed foreign province especially difficult. Allowing only that sovereign to oppose can leave the incumbent church/guild with no defense of its own market. Conversely, a level-zero contact should make entry possible without occupying the land.

**P15:** keep paid Create (0), but type support/opposition: same-type incumbents, and limited Law relevance for secular Guild/Temple actions. Do not let a distant unrelated temple or a level-zero source become a free substitute for a large political base. Require a finite bid/response sequence with committed costs, deterministic ordering, visible reasons and save safety. Positive level growth must still find capacity. No automatic right to tax the province accompanies Create.

### D08 — Development must price both money and political effort

**Consequences:** importing BRCS's 1 GB growth while retaining guaranteed RP-funded PC checks can make wealthy expansion almost certain and very cheap. Keeping PC's target RP cost while adopting every BRCS skill/court bonus may impose the opposite double charge. Province Rule also affects every institution's capacity and available mebhaighl, not just the territorial owner.

**P15:** retain a paid +1 holding action, target-level RP and an explicit recover operation for the initial candidate; evaluate outcomes including the action slot, bids, failures and defender recovery, not only gold. Opposed development should use an agreed typed influence rule shared by UI and AI. Limit province growth attempts per season and require a separately chosen cost/check formula; prohibit an RP-rich realm from instantly urbanizing every province. Never use a ruler's own level as both automatic support and an unexamined difficulty reduction.

For that choice, the current HD province cost is `P+1 GB`, plus `P+1 RP` when P>0, with threshold `10+P−bid`. In BRCS the separate Rule Province heading charges current-level GB, uses `10+P`, disallows RP influence and limits the court to one attempt that season, even if it fails. These are not interchangeable holding-growth rules. The E2 general-resolution worked example gives 16 for P5→P6 despite nearby prose referring to the province rating; keep that ambiguity visible instead of attributing an unqualified exact threshold to the 1995 text.

### D09 — Political disruption versus permanent economic destruction

**Advantages/disadvantages:** two-stage Contest creates warning, response and negotiation; a large holding can still be annihilated in two successes. Permanent 1d3 damage scales the number of attacks needed to dismantle a large institution, but erodes its cash/RP immediately and can produce lengthy rebuilding wars. Combining suppression **and** 1d3 loss would be a third, more punitive rule.

**P15:** keep Sierra's two-stage political conflict candidate; contested holdings earn no income/RP and cannot quietly retain commercial/casting benefits. Give recovery a clear action/cost, track the contesting relationship, and evaluate ongoing attacker presence. Destruction should leave provenance records without turning the tombstone into an active level-zero survival asset. Restrict nonmagical interference with Sources; use supported magical contest rules rather than a fort acting as universal arcane influence. Analyze two-success destruction against war and fortification costs before approving it.

### D10 — Capacity, fragmentation and preserved anomalies

**Consequences:** shared capacity prevents every owner taking H=P, but does not by itself stop several small records from outperforming one consolidated one under nonlinear dice or half-rounded RP. A positive-owner limit is a political-density rule, not the same as aggregate capacity. E2 and BRCS owner thresholds differ; importing one can invalidate existing Anuire records.

**P15:** preserve imported records and grandfathered excess. For production/RP/claim limits, first aggregate records by owner, province and type, while keeping their individual IDs and source links. Apply one economic result to that aggregate and distribute display/provenance afterward. Do not retroactively merge records, delete landless domains or rescale saves. Keep Create (0) possible without fractional production; positive-owner limits require a deliberate scenario-compatible decision and an entry path for new competitors.

The numerical owner-limit difference is explicit: E2's holding discussion groups positive owners at province levels 1–3 / 4–6 / 7–9 as 1 / 2 / 3, with level-zero holdings excluded. B30/B35 Table 5-3 instead prints 1–2 / 3–5 / 6–8 / 9–10 as 1 / 2 / 3 / 4. Source potential supplies the relevant magical capacity. Do not infer a province-10 E2 entry that the inspected passage does not state. HD does not enforce either positive-owner table.

## 5. Shared systems that determine holding value

### D11 — Trade ownership, throughput, profit and RP

**Rules:** E2/PC routes use province-based revenue and thief trade RP; PC-H §250 and HD use terrestrial infrastructure. In BRCS both endpoints need a real guild or negotiated guild partner, positive guild level controls route involvement, each endpoint earns half its own guild level, and trade yields no RP. B30 trade/assets and B35 chapter 5 PDF p. 5 agree on the inspected core. [Components/trade](https://birthright.net/wiki/index.php/BRCS%3AChapter_five/Ruling_a_domain/Components_of_a_domain).

**HD status:** `tradeProblem` explicitly accepts a level-zero origin; no destination guild record is required. Revenue is `floor((P1+P2)/2)` to the route owner, with province endpoint limits, road/passage checks and inactive/destroyed reasons. It does not implement bilateral endpoint profit sharing or sea-route shipping. `finishReport` includes combined trade receipts for thief RP, including ports; that broader RP scope needs an explicit decision.

**Advantages/disadvantages:** province-valued routes reward major markets and political access, but cheaply exploit undeveloped guilds. Guild-limited bilateral trade rewards investment and bargaining; it also needs more contracts, can reduce guilder opening budgets and changes the value of province growth.

**P15 preferred route package:** positive, uncontested real Guild at each endpoint or explicit partner; capacity limited by both provincial market and guild scale; different verified commodity regions/terrain/culture; one bilateral route object and recorded shares; road/port/cargo capacity as applicable. Use the BRCS-style half-guild endpoint return as the first bounded candidate, with a separate decision on a modest thief trade-RP allowance. The conservative initial comparison is **no additional route RP**, retaining ordinary Guild RP. Do not keep full PC route RP automatically after increasing Guild RP/cash or adding free court actions. This is a proposed change to Sierra behavior, requiring the user's decision and opening-budget validation.

### D12 — Sea routes, ports and infrastructure

**Rule differences:** E2 seaport/parts-unknown descriptions differ even within their income/action discussions; do not silently conflate a port asset and a route. PC-H §199, PC-B and HD's approved port policy are explicitly separated in the [port audit](../PORT_INCOME_INVESTIGATION.md). BRCS models ports as infrastructure and adds maintained highways/bridges and shipping requirements; it does not justify minting another full provincial income for every sea connection.

**HD status:** port assets generate the approved full seaport / half river-port GB. Land routes exist; verified sea transport, cargo allocation, blockade and sea-route contracts remain incomplete. Current roads do not have the BRCS recurring construction-maintenance economy.

**P15:** preserve approved port rates and fractional GB. Model route returns as additional bounded commercial profit, not an automatic duplicate of the port asset's full provincial yield. A fleet must not simultaneously carry troops, satisfy several full-capacity routes and earn idle discounts. Charge any new infrastructure costs in the same budget comparison as trade profits. Do not invent missing paired routes or cultural commodity data in the source campaign.

### D13 — Loyalty, Law and religious politics

**Rules/status:** E2's Law-dependent loyalty penalties differ from PC-H §127's normal light/moderate/severe changes and Law/unit protection. HD deliberately follows the help over the modified-source extra low-Law penalty. It queues tax loyalty and applies it during settlement; general loyalty spans runtime 0–4 with source values preserved. Later BRCS uses five attitudes, seasonal d20 adjustment and broad political-religious modifiers; its published attitude table has an unresolved duplicated threshold. [Attitude rules](https://birthright.net/wiki/index.php/BRCS%3AChapter_five/Ruling_a_domain/Adjusting_domain_attitude), B30 pp. 94–95, B35 PDF pp. 6–7.

**Consequences:** replacing coercion immunity with attitude probability changes the value of armies and priests. Importing temple-majority penalties plus Sierra's hard tax/occupation losses can double punish a ruler. A `+1` in one model is not necessarily one loyalty grade in another.

**P15:** keep readable Sierra loyalty grades and one declared set of tax/occupation consequences. Distinguish administrative compliance from religious approval and foreign-dynasty hostility. Law can protect collection without making the populace love repression. Temple/Guild influence should affect relevant agitation, events and negotiations through bounded modifiers; do not stack several versions of the same grievance. Specify one delayed-effect boundary and test its interaction with own occupation and severe taxation.

The important exact PC-H/HD protection rule is two negative grades ignored with full effective Law, one with at least half, none below half; occupying companies replace Law for this purpose. The normal tax directions are Light +1, Moderate 0, Severe −1, before occupation and protection. E2's low-Law passage instead describes Moderate −1 and Severe −2, while its ordinary adjustment list rewards **no tax**, not simply every light-tax selection. This is a genuine difference, not wording to blend. B30/B35's optional variable-tax rule changes income by 1 GB decrees, at most one change per season and no increase above 50% of base; it feeds its attitude model. HD has not implemented that variant.

### D14 — Occupation, pillage and destruction of institutions

**Rules/status:** PC-H §§114/115/117 permits own occupation, troop-limited pillage and separate holding reduction; it describes foreign Law/Guild/Temple falling to zero and Source losing a level. The modified source has different allied/source handling, so those are not identical confirmations. Current HD implements hostile military control, 1 GB/province expense, no provincial RP, immediate free pillage once per province/season, and independent surviving holdings. Own-occupation and staged management pillage remain disabled.

The later BRCS Occupy action instead requires stationary troops for a month, temporary Law influence and gradual destruction. B30 p. 106, B35 PDF p. 17, [Occupy action](https://birthright.net/wiki/index.php/Contest_holding). This is materially different from an immediate permanent zeroing of every institution.

**Advantages/disadvantages:** instant reduction gives the army a decisive role but can replace years of political investment with one cheap checkbox. Slow destruction gives institutions time to negotiate or fortify; it demands a real movement/calendar model and can bog down conquest.

**P15:** occupation is coercive control, not title. Require a sustained garrison and a separately committed suppression/destruction operation with action/time, loyalty and diplomatic costs. Prefer bounded per-month mundane holding damage over instant province-wide erasure. Sources resist ordinary military confiscation; deliberate magical/despoiling effects need explicit rules. Pillage changes province/holding capacity and treasury once, against an immutable collection ledger; choosing it before versus after collection must not create reroll/cancel/double-tax arbitrage. Selection/commit timing remains a user decision, not implemented by this audit.

### D15 — Ley lines and source geography

**Rules:** E2's Forge Ley Line has RP upkeep; later BRCS removes recurring upkeep but adds distance to casting cost, permits destinations without a local Source and uses a specified geometric path. Both use the strongest linked power rather than adding several weak Sources. B30 pp. 108–109 and inherited chapter 7; B35 PDF p. 19. [Arcane geography](https://birthright.net/wiki/index.php/BRCS%3AChapter_seven/Realm_magic/Arcane_realm_magic).

**HD status:** both endpoints must be owned uncontested Sources, including level zero; paths follow directed original province adjacency, three outgoing slots, no recurring upkeep. Contest/ownership mismatch suspends links; destroyed endpoints destroy them. Network access takes the maximum connected source, not a sum. Source development follows recovered potential with an elven exception; province growth can trim Sources. These are functional paths with explicit compatibility limits.

**P15:** preserve original geography and maximum-not-sum access. Keep an actual endpoint Source (0) requirement for readable map control. Choose **one** expansion restraint: modest per-link RP upkeep, or a clearly displayed distance surcharge per cast; the preferred candidate is use-based distance cost, which makes remote wars expensive without bankrupting inactive mages. Never remove both upkeep and distance cost by mixing editions. State who can oppose a route through their Sources; ownership of the land alone should not give universal control of mebhaighl. Do not use terrain painting or arbitrary straight-line coordinates to change the simulation graph.

### D16 — Investiture, vassalage and institutional transfers

**Rules/status:** E2 Investiture is priest-mediated and can cover holdings as well as provinces. Sierra's occupation/garrison investment and its priest discount are not identical to that ritual requirement. Current HD province investment changes sovereignty without moving independent holdings, reserves or surviving third-party forts. Landed vassals retain their own slots/resources; non-landed assimilation retains provenance and transfers institutions. BRCS uses named Ceremony procedures plus specified divine support for relevant ceremonies; routine transfer, conquest and vassalage are not interchangeable actions. B30 pp. 106–108, B35 PDF p. 18; PC-H §§150/151/258.

**Consequences:** automatic institutional annexation makes armies a cheap substitute for Guild/Temple/Source competition. Conversely, ceremonial costs on every ordinary commercial transaction can make bargaining impractical. A vassal's RP payment can exploit collection/reserve caps if credited before its payer is actually charged, or forwarded through a chain repeatedly.

Current HD's ordinary provincial investment attempt costs 1 GB with an eligible priest or 4 GB without, provincial-level RP plus an optional 0–9 RP bid, one action, and a seeded d20 against `10−bid`, after occupation/garrison checks. It therefore neither requires a universal temple holding nor models every priest-mediated E2 holding-transfer ritual. Investiture opposition and full character modifiers remain incomplete.

**P15:** preserve separate title, military control and institution ownership. Define consensual transfer, contested divestiture, conquest investment, inheritance and vassal tribute as distinct operations. Preserve Temple's ritual/diplomatic value without making every willing contract a province conquest. Use explicit conserved payments from each actor's own resources; no phantom percentage, double collection or circular re-lending. A landless domain remains a real participant. The exact religious gate, RP pledge and succession rules require a user-selected specification.

### D17 — Fortifications and protected institutions

**Rules:** E2 differentiates fortified holdings and province castles, with different build costs; Sierra adds independently owned castles/towers and specific siege/neutralization rules. BRCS Table 5-4 scales holding/province fort costs and upkeep by level, but inherited warfare prose conflicts with its maintenance model. That conflict is acknowledged in [publisher errata](https://birthright.net/wiki/index.php/Errata); it cannot be resolved by claiming one universal "3.5 maintenance rate."

**HD status:** individual castles/towers have owner/level/construction state; province summaries are derived. Support costs per fort asset, not per level; unpaid forts decay. The complete fortified Law/Guild/Temple protection model, seizure immunity and all siege/transfer interactions do not exist merely because castle records exist. Aggregate old saves cannot be split into invented castles.

The principal numeric comparison is 8 GB per castle/province-fort level versus 4 GB per fortified-holding level. The inspected BRCS Table 5-4 maintenance rates are 2/3 GB per province-fort level and 1/3 GB per holding-fort level; these must be labeled that table's rates, alongside the contrary inherited prose. Current HD instead charges 1 GB per supported individual fort and progresses ordinary castle construction by seeded 1d6 GB per season toward its target.

**P15:** distinguish castle, tower and fortified institution. Keep IDs/ownership independent. Select build cost, effective protected levels, upkeep and suppression/occupation consequences as one package. A protected institution should resist seizure and require siege or a separate political/magical counter, but fortification must not make its commercial contracts or public legitimacy untouchable. Compare flat-per-fort versus level-scaled maintenance at low and high levels; do not combine cheap flat upkeep with unlimited high-level immunity unnoticed.

### D18 — Actions, court administration and lieutenants

**Rules/status:** Sierra/HD have three interleaved paid slots plus documented free orders. Later BRCS splits standard, character, court and realm actions; court size can multiply administrative work and multi-target scope. This is a new throughput economy, not just another label for the same three actions. [Action rounds](https://birthright.net/wiki/index.php/BRCS%3AChapter_five/Ruling_a_domain/Domain_action_rounds). HD has controlled vassal slots and only partial lieutenant mechanics; Basic/Advanced/Expert are not implemented as equivalent original modes and remain distinct from rules profiles and faction availability.

**Consequences:** cheaper development plus extra court/lieutenant actions plus merchant income compounds far faster than either edition's isolated formula suggests. Passive alt domains can become action factories. A PC free action and a BRCS court action are not equivalent costs.

**P15:** preserve three meaningful strategic rounds for the initial campaign. Price any delegated action by actor, resources, eligibility and a bounded shared administration budget. Do not grant unlimited extra development per lieutenant or per assimilated realm. Basic may automate chosen rules and Expert expose more decisions, but arbitrary safety income and automatic investment must be separately chosen, not inferred from the words Basic/Expert. Their implementation is deferred until the audit's decisions are made.

### D19 — Seasonal processing, spells and forecast integrity

**HD status:** `finishSeason` expires effects and processes construction/siege/diplomacy before Council and turn advancement. `CompleteCouncil` runs standard events/NPC administration, then Maintenance; Collect Taxes commits all-realm actual income once; Select Actions pays expenses and collects RP from the stored ledger. A pre-existing collection report and a forecast of a modified state are different objects. See the [current settlement contract](../MAINTENANCE_SETTLEMENT_CONTRACT.md).

**Important inspection finding:** a normal one-duration action-phase income spell receives `expiresTurn = turn + 1`; `finishSeason` filters that effect before the next collection, and `hasMagicEffect` also requires `expiresTurn > state.turn`. That path appears capable of removing Bless Land/Blight before their intended next Maintenance benefit. Direct-settlement tests are not proof that the full phase journey works. This is a code-traced risk requiring a focused reproduction before any fix, not a claim based on a new browser run.

**P15:** specify an explicit seasonal calendar and collection entitlement for each spell, occupation and loyalty effect. An income modifier should survive exactly its promised number of eligible collections; movement wards can use a different action-round expiry. Costs must use receipts actually available at the selected commit boundary. Forecasting, opening reports, rejected commands and save/load must not consume RNG or replay income. Preserve versioned old histories; no silent rescaling or retroactive collection.

### D20 — Failure, bankruptcy, data defaults and exceptional actors

**Rules/status:** PC-H §167 describes adjusting management choices before terminal insolvency. HD instead applies compulsory expense priority, affordable court reduction, fort decay, troop GB/RP fallback, unpaid consequences and `bankrupt` outcome checks. Native event-controller/NPC fallback paths are separate. BRCS permits negotiated obligations and its own bloodline/expense consequences, not a license for the same player to spend both tomorrow's RP and today's full reserve.

**P15:** show selected costs, actual balances, reserved obligations and the consequence of every shortfall. Require manual correction of explicit unaffordable player choices before finalization; keep any optional automation visible and predictable. Preserve deliberate unpaid troops/unsupported forts as actual decisions. Define terminal bankruptcy independently from a recoverable deficit. Never label special NPC grants as ordinary holding production or distribute them to all factions. Missing imported skills/books/records remain unknown, not zero or optimally filled.

## 10. Current status and limits

This research establishes a source-located comparison and a proposed 1.5 direction. It does not verify every Sierra executable, resolve all historical table ambiguities or prove a final campaign balance. The [economic cases](BIRTHRIGHT_15_HOLDINGS_ECONOMIC_CASES.md) are transparent analytical examples and code-path risks, not fresh completed gameplay regressions. Proposed values, missing subsystems and questions requiring user judgment are kept separate from current implementation claims.

No TypeScript, simulation schema, importer, campaign data, artwork, save or gameplay behavior was changed. M1B remains partial. Its next major economic implementation is gated on this audit and the user's selected consistent specification.

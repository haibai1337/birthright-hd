# M1C-1 — Anuire illustrated strategic map

The design/review baseline is **1366×768 at 100% browser zoom**. The original **1920×2234** world, all 206 province geometries and seven multi-ring provinces are unchanged. Larger viewports respond naturally; the surrounding parchment/leather interface and mechanics are retained.

## Rendering

The existing Pixi renderer now separates native base art, painted ground material, source-guided environmental objects, water/coasts, constructed roads, ownership/borders, buildings, forces, labels and selection. Objects have ground anchors and depth ordering. The initial M1C-1 version had **253 forest groups, 123 ridge groups and five hill groups**; M1C-1B corrects their placement and proportions (below). These are varied painted raster masses, rather than geometric terrain icons.

Terrain anchors are deterministic visual approximations derived from original map pixels and recovered terrain categories. The retail palette is shared by sea and mountain strokes; connectivity supplies a water mask, but cannot by itself identify mountain geography. Coastlines and river paths come from the original image; no new geographic paths or province edges are authored. Appearance uses an independent hash, never the campaign PRNG.

Static illustration/geometry is built once. Atlas frames share GPU textures, offscreen objects are culled, text textures are reused, and panning/zooming updates the camera rather than rebuilding the world. Zoom animates toward the pointer; dragging is bounded. The original image remains at native resolution. Whole-world fit uses a water backdrop rather than gray margins.

**Relief / Original** switches at the same camera coordinates. **Realm tint** is optional; terrain is not flooded with opaque political colors. Borders and selection use the same original rings as even-odd hit-testing. Broad views favor realm names; closer views expose province names and more markers. Hover/selection remain identifiable.

## M1C-1B: geographic placement correction

**Actual causes.** The old 78-pixel jittered grid averaged rock over a 70-pixel square: anchors could sit between small rock strokes while narrow forested ridges fell below its threshold. All three native mountain provinces in Five Peaks (The Gorge, Floodspaeth, Puinol) received zero ridge groups. Taeghas used 135-161-pixel alpine masses on much smaller inland features; their 0.76 vertical anchor displaced the artwork north as well as spreading it over neighboring lowlands. Its forest/plain fallback also accepted rock without a province, generating offshore mountains from gray sea-edge dithering (for example RGB 123/123/123 was rock but not water). Pink ground alone generated sandstone ranges, and the hill branch incorrectly used rocky frames 4-7 instead of gentle frames 10-11. Thus palette ambiguity was one coastal failure, not an adequate explanation for Five Peaks.

**Correction.** `terrain-placement.ts` uses 2-pixel integral masks, local 20-pixel rock evidence, 12-pixel candidate spacing and strongest-feature spacing. Native terrain constrains prominence; small source-supported boundary outcrops can cross into another category without becoming alpine masses. Sandstone requires local shadow/highlight structure and highland context. Water connectivity includes gray coastal dithering, rejects isolated gray peaks as lakes, and the complete ground footprint is checked near water. No environmental anchor is generated outside the 206 recovered province geometries. Relief now centers on the extracted feature instead of shifting north from it.

Two editable, source-referenced outlines in `terrain-regions.ts` constrain exceptional scale: the narrow inland Taeghas/Avanil range and the depicted northern Five Peaks chain. They cannot create mountains without local image evidence. Portage/Bayside remain clear; Seamist, Bhaine and Brosien retain small inland rocky forms (the original records classify these three as mountains). Nentril/Vanilen retain distinct high mountains. The Gorge, Floodspaeth and Puinol receives three prominent source-aligned groups, with surrounding forest and the separate southeastern spur retained. Rhuobhe and previously missed highland provinces now receive local relief. Gorgon's Crown/Markazor/Mur-Kilad follow their rough pink ridges rather than filling every pink plain; Baruk-Azhik and neighboring Chimaeron keep their eastern ranges. Spiderfell and the major forest belts retain canopy.

Forests now vary width and height from local green/shadow coverage: small woods and edges are lower and smaller than dense forest. Nearby peaks reduce canopy overlap. Existing raster atlases/materials, source artwork, all 206 polygons, world dimensions, gameplay terrain, movement and simulation remain unchanged. The revised map has **203 forest, 75 ridge and 34 hill groups**.

Run `node scripts/audit-terrain-placement.mjs` with the local Vite server and private original assets available. It writes `test-results/m1c1b/terrain-audit.json`: all 206 province counts, missing highland/oversized lowland warnings, source evidence, independent repeatability, and a short list of small cross-category outcrops for review. Current result: all 33 native mountain/high-mountain provinces have relief; zero oversized lowland ridges, zero anchors outside provinces. Seven small outcrop cases remain explicitly listed, including Seasdeep and Wilder's Gorge. This checks placement constraints, not pixel-perfect geography.

Reviewed real browser captures in `test-results/m1c1b/` are all **1366x768, DPR 1, 100% zoom**. Identical-camera Original/Relief pairs cover Taeghas, Five Peaks, Avanil, the northeast and eastern highlands; `04-anuire-relief-1366x768.png` shows the whole map. `review.json` records camera equality, actual canvas selection, real army movement, no browser errors and scene reuse during pan. Six focused terrain tests pass; one `npm run build` passed TypeScript and production browser output. Placement analysis runs once during scene creation (0.3-1.5 seconds across local read-only runs); fewer objects are drawn than before (312 versus 381), and panning reuses the static scene. No broad regression suite was run.

**Remaining uncertainty:** the retail artwork supplies no elevation field. Exact heights, minor isolated crags and the transition from a small rocky feature to a hill are visual interpretations. Native categories and source locations are evidence; remaster silhouette sizes are not canonical measurements. M1C-2 has not started.
